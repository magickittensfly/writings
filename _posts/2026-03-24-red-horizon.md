title: "Red Horizon"
---

At the temple entrance, the four of them gathered. 
James unrolled the letter with careful fingers as they stepped toward the western gate, the next path leading away from the golden light of Cindrelune and into a harsher, dustier landscape.
Julie slung her bag over her shoulder. “Who’s next?” she asked.
James scanned the parchment. “Robert Greaves. He’s stationed in the old stone city of Ovestra.”
At the name, Beverley said nothing. Her eyes flicked briefly toward James as he spoke, but her expression remained unreadable.
Julie glanced over, then added, “I know of him. He is in the Stone Guard military unit. I’m surprised you don’t know him, James.”
James shook his head. “Never worked with the Stone Guard. That’s a specialized corps, different command structure.”
Shively stretched his arms and offered a half-yawn, half-thought. “It’s not just specialized. I’ve heard only certain bloodlines are allowed to serve in the Stone Guard. Some old rite tied to the founding of Ovestra. Not sure if that’s rumor or truth, but… it’d be nice to find out.”
Julie grinned. “You just want something juicy to write down in your scrolls.”
Shively gave a mock bow. “Of course. Lore is everything.”
 
They passed beyond the outer gates of Cindrelune, the light behind them dimming into the morning haze. Ahead, the road turned rocky, veined with old cobblestone and dust.
Somewhere far ahead, Robert waited. The heat was relentless. Their boots sank into soft sand and cracked earth until finally, after what felt like an endless day, they reached a modest oasis tucked between two sun-scorched ridges.
Julie dropped to the ground near the water, groaning. “Why didn’t we bring horses? Or mules? I didn’t think the distance between Cindrelune and Ovestra was this long.”

James crouched beside her, wiping sweat from his brow. “Maybe horses would’ve been a good idea. But I don’t own any. Do you?”

She shook her head, still panting.

James turned to Shively. “What about you?”

Shively gave an apologetic shrug. “Nope. I have scrolls, not saddles.”

James then looked at Beverley, who was quietly refilling her flask at the water’s edge. “Do you own any horses?”

She glanced at him with an unreadable expression, then answered flatly, “No.”

James blinked. It was the first time he’d heard her speak. For a while, he’d wondered if she’d taken a vow of silence like some temple healers. But no, she simply didn’t speak unless necessary.

“Well,” he muttered, “that settles it. None of us own horses. That’s why we’re walking.”

Julie gave a soft laugh and rolled onto her back, staring at the clouds. Shively crouched beside her, making sure she drank. She noticed, though she didn’t say anything, and slid just a little closer to him on the grass.

Beverley, by contrast, looked almost untouched by the heat. No sweat, no complaints. She moved quietly around the oasis, refilling not just her own containers, but everyone else’s without a word.

James watched her for a moment. She handed him water, as if it was instinct.

As they cooled off and rationed supplies, the silence between them was not tense. They all knew that this was only a pause. The desert would stretch another day. And another oasis was unlikely.

Nightfall came swiftly, and with it, a biting cold. None of them had sleeping bags or even proper cloaks for warmth. The oasis offered some comfort, soft grass patches and broad, leathery leaves from the lone miracle tree that had somehow taken root there.

Beverley, quiet as ever, was the one who set up their modest camp. She layered the giant leaves over the grass, fashioning a sleeping space. But when she finished, it was clear, there was only room for three.

“I can stay up, or sleep against the tree, no problem,” James offered, already scanning the tree for a good lean.

Beverley shook her head, just once, but before she could respond, Shively jumped in.

“I’m more than okay sleeping on the sand next to you guys, no worries. I’ve gone days without sleep,could even keep one eye open if it helps.”

Julie glanced at the small patch of grass, then at Shively. “Well, Shiv,” she said “we could share. If I lie on my side, we’ll both fit. Just don’t snore in my ear.”

Shively gave a mock salute. “No promises.”

The desert, blazing by day, had turned cruel by night. The cold radiated up from the sand, made worse by the proximity to water. They all instinctively began to huddle in, seeking warmth.

James, ever the analyst, scanned the arrangement one last time before settling. Beverley had placed herself between him and Julie, perhaps intentionally, perhaps not. Julie and Shively curled near each other, sharing space like it wasn’t their first time doing so. Something about it felt familiar.

James found a spot half on the grass, half on the sand. Not ideal, but manageable. Beverley had given him a folded tunic to use as a pillow, without a word.

As they all tucked in, the silence was thick with fatigue. But there was a strange comfort in it. This journey had no room for distance, physical or otherwise.

Tomorrow, they’d reach Ovestra. But tonight, they lay beneath the stars, four strangers trying to stay warm.
